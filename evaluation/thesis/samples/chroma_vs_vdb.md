3. **Level 2 (Geophysical Parameters)**:
   - **Atmospheric Correction**: Remove the effects of atmospheric conditions (e.g., clouds, aerosols) to retrieve surface reflectance or other geophysical parameters.

   - **Temporal Compositing**: Combine multiple observations over a period to create composite images that reduce the effects of clouds or other temporal variations.
   - **Quality Control**: Ensure the data meets quality standards, including validation against ground truth measurements.

5. **Level 4 (Model Outputs)**:
   - **Data Assimilation**: Integrate satellite data with numerical models to produce higher-level products such as weather forecasts, climate projections, or environmental assessments.

4. **Fractional Coverage by Cloud Types**: Instead of using total fractional area coverage by clouds, which is a relatively poor predictor, dividing the coverage into contributions from several distinct cloud types provides a much better prediction of radiation budget quantities. This approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of how different cloud types contribute to the overall radiation balance.

5. **Regression Equations**: The regression equations derived from satellite data can estimate the net effect of clouds on the radiation balance and identify the specific contributions from particular types of clouds.
